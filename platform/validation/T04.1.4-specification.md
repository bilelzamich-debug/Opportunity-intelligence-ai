## 3. Scope

**In scope:** ordinal band scales for Problem `severity` and `frequency`;
the rating representation on `Problem` and `InferenceRequest`; the
evidence-linkage (traceability) mechanism for ratings; the **evidence
ceiling** — the maximum defensible rating derivable from the available
Facts — enforced by the engine before any state change and by P-V4/P-I4 on
the acceptance/detective surfaces; the versioned-path rule for weight
changes; M-12's closure status.

**Out of scope:** §14 (non-goals) — population scales, taxonomy (M-21),
dedup (M-22), pattern thresholds, quantitative scales, rating
auto-derivation, semantic verification of band appropriateness, new modules,
store/lifecycle/contract changes, governance updates pre-ratification.

## 4. Source reconciliation — what is ratified vs. what this specification must decide

**Ratified and binding (not open to T04.1.4):** Principle 1; N-4 (the
engine contributes no content — a rating is inferer-supplied and only
verified); S-4 (independence-based sufficiency, Problem floor 2); S-2 P6;
R-3 (confidence is the axis that carries corroboration, separate from
content semantics); N-16 Tier-1 derivation; R-1/V11 (weight revision is a
versioning trigger); N-10 (closed stage set); P-V4/P-I4 as the
acceptance/detective anchor points; the T04.1.2/T04.1.3 enforcement pattern
(engine refuses before any state change; acceptance rules stay
authoritative; the detective covers paths no single write controls).

**Pre-analyzed but NOT ratified (B-37):** the choice of ordinal bands with
evidence-linked justification (Option 4). The backlog adopts its wording;
the ratification of the *specific* semantics remains to be done — that is
precisely what WD-1…WD-6 are for.

**Interpretation A (presented for ratification as part of WD-1/WD-2):**
B-37's "consistent with the confidence banding in D-03" is read as
**mechanism consistency, not count matching** — bands defined by observable
criteria with worked anchors (the S-01 style) — because confidence is a
calibrated continuous measure needing O2-correction granularity, while
severity/frequency are categorical characterisations. S-01's five-band count
is a property of confidence calibration, not of ordinal banding generally.

**Interpretation B:** B-37's note that M-22 (dedup) "should be co-decided"
concerns the blocker *analysis* (B-37/B-38 were analysed together); the
ratified sequencing authority is the backlog, which runs `T04.1.4` (weight)
before `T04.1.5` (dedup). The weight scale does not depend on identity
semantics: dedup affects which Problems are the *same*, not what a Problem's
weight *means*. No dependency is taken on T04.1.5.

**Confirmed absence:** no document in the repository defines a severity or
frequency band vocabulary, count, or evidence mapping (searched
`oip/`, `tests/`, `docs/`, `decisions/`; the only "band" matches are the
backlog task title, its AC line, B-37's options, and S-01's confidence
rubric). The bands are therefore a **design decision requiring
ratification**, not a recovered requirement.

- `FactContribution(fact_ref, contribution)` (~@224) and

- `pv4_weight_present` (~@583) — presence only; detail text "severity and

  exists yet -- M-12 is open and the bands land at T04.1.4."
- `_check_pi4` (~@871) — P-I4 detective, **three mechanical bounds**:
  (1) declared `independent_source_count` ≤ sum of supporting Facts' own
  counts [N-16, S-4]; (2) ≤ distinct Evidence beneath those Facts [N-16];
  (3) `evidential_support` ≤ contributing Facts' support [S-2 P6].
  Docstring: "Severity and frequency themselves are NOT compared: no scale
  exists (M-12), and inventing an ordering here would pre-empt T04.1.4."

### 5.2 The engine (`platform/oip/inference.py`)

- Engine scope docstring (~@85–97): "Severity/frequency bands (T04.1.4,
  M-12) … deliberately absent. The engine … **never ranks weight** …".
- `InferenceRequest` (~@360): `severity: str`, `frequency: str` required,
  never defaulted — "Every field the Problem requires is REQUIRED here and
  never defaulted … The engine contributes no content of its own [N-4]."
- Engine-derived evidence (N-16 Tier-1, ~@956–995): resolves the supporting

  Evidence that does not resolve is a refusal (`EVIDENCE_UNRESOLVED`), never
  a silent zero. Non-ACTIVE Evidence contributes no key.
- Explanation text (~@1096): "Severity and frequency are free text: no
  scales exist yet (M-12 open, bands at T04.1.4)".
- `Problem` construction (~@1116–1129): `severity=request.severity`,

- `InferenceStage` (N-10 closed set, ~@160–182): … `INSUFFICIENT_SOURCES`,
  `TEMPORAL_CONFLICT`, `STORE_REJECTED`, plus the T04.1.2 versioned-path

  a documented closed-set extension with an attempted-stage classification.
- The T04.1.3 population gate runs **before any state change** on the
  versioned path; a refusal leaves the predecessor ACTIVE and nothing

### 5.4 Confirmed gaps (= exactly the task)

1. **No band scales** — severity/frequency are unconstrained free text.
2. **No evidence linkage** — a rating is not traceable to Facts; nothing
   checks that the asserted weight rests on the supporting set.
3. **No evidence ceiling** — P-I4 compares only the numeric measures;
   the ordinal weight claim is unbounded (`severity="EXTREME"` passes on a
   2-source Problem).
4. **No engine gate** — the weight judgement is absent from the inference
   path entirely (the numeric bounds are detective-only, never enforced at
   write time).
5. **M-12 unresolved** — no ratified scale exists.

### WD-1 / Decision A — Severity bands

The severity scale characterises **how damaging the problem is when it
occurs** (IOM ~@817). Rating is inferer-supplied (N-4); the bands below are
calibration vocabulary with observable criteria in the S-01 style; the
engine bounds them via the WD-4 evidence ceiling, never derives them.

**Option A-1 — three bands (RECOMMENDED, DRAFT).**

| Band | Observable criterion (what the linked Facts must explicitly evidence) | Worked anchor |
|---|---|---|
| `MINOR` | Friction or obstacle with at-most-recoverable cost: lost time, effort, workaround overhead; no stated lasting damage | Users must re-enter a form after a session timeout |
| `MODERATE` | Stated recurring or compounding cost: money, data quality, customer or productivity loss with stated scope | Orders silently fail reconciliation, requiring monthly manual corrections |
| `SEVERE` | Stated irreversible or category-defining harm: safety, health, legal exposure, permanent data loss | A payment path double-charges a subset of customers |

- Number of bands: 3. Single criterion type: **the most severe harm
  category explicitly evidenced in the linked Facts** (recoverable →
  compounding → irreversible).
- **The criteria are content-based, not count-based:** which band a Problem
  can claim is a function of *what the evidence states*, never of *how much
  evidence there is*. Corroboration plays no part in the criteria; its role
  is fixed by WD-4 (sufficiency and confidence) and by R-3 (the confidence
  axis). This is the load-bearing separation of Example 5 (§WD-4.4).
- Advantages: fewest boundary disputes; coarsest scale consistent with
  ordinal banding; lowest false-precision risk (B-37's accepted trade-off
  is minimised); still aggregatable for Pattern Intelligence (P5's
  frequency-inflation narrative needs ordinal comparability, not
  granularity).
- Disadvantages: cannot express "moderate-to-severe" borderline cases; two
  problems with materially different damage land in the same band.
- Effect on evidence traceability: each band's criterion is stated over the
  *linked* Facts, so the rating's justification (WD-3) names exactly which
  Facts evidence the harm category; traceability is direct.
- Risk of false precision: **lowest** of the three options.

**Option A-2 — four bands.** `MINOR / MODERATE / MAJOR / CRITICAL` — splits
A-1's top band into stated compounding operational/financial harm (`MAJOR`)
versus stated irreversible harm (`CRITICAL`).
- Advantages: separates the qualitatively distinct "irreversible" category
  from "large but recoverable", which P5 scoring may eventually need.
- Disadvantages: the `MAJOR`/`CRITICAL` and `MODERATE`/`MAJOR` boundaries
  are the two most contestable judgements in severity rating; one more
  criterion boundary to dispute and to evidence.
- Traceability: same mechanism, one more category to evidence.
- Risk of false precision: moderate.

**Option A-3 — five bands.** `NEGLIGIBLE / MINOR / MODERATE / MAJOR /
EXTREME` — mirrors S-01's five-band granularity.
- Advantages: granularity parity with the confidence rubric; finest
  aggregation resolution.
- Disadvantages: S-01's count exists for O2 calibration correction of a
  continuous measure; severity is a categorical judgement, so the extra
  rungs manufacture distinctions the evidence cannot ground — precisely
  B-37's "false precision" warning; three internal boundaries to dispute.
- Traceability: unchanged mechanism; more bands over the same evidence
  thins the anchor set.
- Risk of false precision: **highest**.

**Recommendation (NOT RATIFIED): A-1.** Most consistent with B-37 Option 4
(ordinal, evidence-linked, no quantitative veneer), S-01's *style* (one
observable criterion type, worked anchors) without copying its
calibration-motivated count (Interpretation A, §4), Principle 1 and
conservative evidence semantics. The owner may prefer A-2 if P5 scoring is
expected to need the reversible/irreversible split sooner.

### WD-2 / Decision B — Frequency bands

The frequency scale characterises **how often the problem occurs for the
affected population**. As with severity: inferer-supplied, observable
criteria, bounded by the WD-4 evidence ceiling, never engine-derived.

**Option B-1 — three bands (RECOMMENDED, DRAFT).**

| Band | Observable criterion (what the linked Facts must explicitly evidence) | Worked anchor |
|---|---|---|
| `EPISODIC` | Occurrence is evidenced, without evidence of repetition or continuity | A migration tool failed for one customer on one run |
| `RECURRING` | Repeated occurrence is evidenced — the same deficiency arises again across instances or contexts | The reconciliation failure is reported every month-end close |
| `PERSISTENT` | Continuous or unabating occurrence is evidenced — the deficiency is present whenever the process runs | Every checkout on the affected payment path double-charges |

- Number of bands: 3. Single criterion type: **the recurrence character
  evidenced** (occurrence → repetition → continuity).
- **The criteria are content-based, not count-based** (as WD-1): how often
  a problem occurs is evidenced by what the linked Facts state about
  recurrence, never by how many sources or Facts there are. Corroboration's
  role is fixed by WD-4 and R-3.
- Advantages: mirrors WD-1's granularity (one mental model); frequency
  claims are exactly the claims the P5 frequency-inflation narrative
  (PT-V2) needs to police, and three ordinal values are sufficient to
  detect inflation of `EPISODIC → PERSISTENT`.
- Disadvantages: cannot express rate information (deliberately — B-37
  rejected quantitative measures); "recurring" without a rate is coarse.
- Evidence requirements: repetition/continuity must be evidenced in the
  linked Facts and cited in the rating's justification (WD-3); the WD-4
  ceiling bounds what is assertable.
- Risk of false precision: lowest.

**Option B-2 — four bands.** `ISOLATED / EPISODIC / RECURRING / CHRONIC` —
adds a true single-occurrence bottom band below "episodic".
- Advantages: distinguishes "happened once" from "happens occasionally".
- Disadvantages: the `ISOLATED`/`EPISODIC` boundary is the weakest
  observable distinction available (both are "occurrence without shown
  repetition"); one more criterion boundary for the least evidence-grounded
  split.
- Evidence requirements: as B-1 plus the isolated/occasional distinction,
  which the evidence rarely grounds.
- Risk of false precision: moderate.

**Option B-3 — five bands.** `RARE / OCCASIONAL / FREQUENT / REGULAR /
CONTINUOUS` — near-quantitative rate ordering.
- Advantages: closest to how stakeholders speak about frequency.
- Disadvantages: the names imply rates the evidence does not state; every
  adjacent pair is a disguised rate boundary — this is Option 3 of B-37
  (quantitative measures) reached by vocabulary, and B-37 rejected it
  ("rarely traceable to Facts and would invite unfounded precision").
- Risk of false precision: **highest**; effectively disrecommended by B-37
  itself.

**Recommendation (NOT RATIFIED): B-1** — the most conservative
evidence-grounded design: every band boundary is an observable recurrence
character, no band name implies a rate, and the scale is exactly as coarse
as the evidence can support.

### WD-3 / Decision C — Rating representation

How a banded rating with evidence linkage is carried on `InferenceRequest`
and `Problem` (today: bare free-text `str` on both, carried verbatim).

**Option C-1 — replace free text with structured band data.**
`severity: WeightBand`, `frequency: WeightBand` (no detail, no per-rating
citations).
- Compatibility: breaking — every construction site and fixture.
- API impact: `Problem.severity`/`frequency` type change `str → WeightBand`;
  P-V4 rewritten.
- Fixture impact: all free-text fixtures rewritten.
- Migration: no persisted object data exists at this phase (pre-P1; the
  store is runtime-constructed), so migration is confined to tests.
- Evidence-linkage implications: **fails AC-2** — "each rating traceable to
  Facts" has no carrier; linkage would have to live in the P-V5 basis,
  which justifies the *inference*, not the *weight*. Rejected primarily on
  this.

**Option C-2 — structured rating preserving detail text (RECOMMENDED,
DRAFT; amended in Rev. 2 for the WD-4 ceiling).**

```
WeightBand        — str-Enum per axis (WD-1/WD-2 vocabularies)
WeightCriterion   — str-Enum per axis, bijection with the bands:
                      severity:  RECOVERABLE_FRICTION / COMPOUNDING_COST / IRREVERSIBLE_HARM
                      frequency: OCCURRENCE / REPETITION / CONTINUITY
WeightContribution — frozen dataclass (the weight analogue of FactContribution):
                      fact_ref: str          # a supporting Fact
                      criterion: WeightCriterion   # what this Fact evidences toward the rating
                      contribution: str      # the inferer's statement of what the Fact establishes
WeightRating      — frozen dataclass:
                      band: WeightBand
                      detail: str                              # preserved free text
                      justification: tuple[WeightContribution, ...]
Problem.severity / .frequency         : WeightRating   (type change str → WeightRating)
InferenceRequest.severity / .frequency: WeightRating   (same shape, N-4 no-default)
```

- **Rev. 2 amendment:** under the Rev. 1 WD-4 (corroboration ladder) the
  justification entries were plain `FactContribution`s; the reworked WD-4
  evidence ceiling requires each entry to declare **which band criterion
  the cited Fact evidences**, so `WeightContribution` adds the typed
  `criterion` field. The amendment is part of this same DRAFT and is
  ratified (or not) together with WD-4.
- The `detail` field preserves exactly today's information content (the IOM
  example `severity: "HIGH — unnoticed loss reaches end customers"` becomes
  `WeightRating(band=…, detail="unnoticed loss reaches end customers",
  justification=…)`).
- The `justification` mirrors the ratified **P-V5 `FactContribution`
  precedent** (fact_ref + what it establishes), applied to weight and
  extended with the criterion declaration. No new linkage concept beyond
  the typed criterion.
- Compatibility: breaking at the type level (constructors, fixtures);
  information-preserving (nothing today expressible is lost).
- API impact: `Problem`/`InferenceRequest` field type changes; `infer()`
  signature unchanged (fields change type, not shape); P-V4 extended
  (valid band, valid criteria for the axis, non-empty detail, non-empty
  justification, every cited fact_ref ∈ `supporting_facts`); new engine
  ceiling gate (WD-4).
- Fixture impact: every fixture carrying free-text severity/frequency —
  the breadth is the main regression lever (§13); migration is mechanical.
- Evidence-linkage implications: AC-2 becomes mechanically checkable —
  subset rule (cited ⊆ supporting), non-empty rule, per-axis rule, and the
  criterion declarations make the WD-4 ceiling computable.
- Migration implications: no persisted data; test-only migration.

**Option C-3 — keep `severity: str` free text and add parallel structured
fields** (`severity_band`, `severity_justification`, …).
- Compatibility: apparently maximal — old strings keep satisfying presence.
- API impact: `Problem` grows three-plus optional fields; P-V4 must then
  require the structured fields anyway (AC-1/AC-2), so old-style requests
  fail the new P-V4 regardless — the compatibility is illusory.
- Fixture impact: same migration as C-2 plus dead fields.
- Migration: dual representation of one fact ("severity" string vs
  `severity_band`) — a standing ambiguity the acceptance rules must then
  police forever; violates the single-source-of-truth reading of AD-02's
  finite interface surface.
- Evidence-linkage: same as C-2 but attached to a redundant field.
- A sub-variant — keep `str` and parse a band token prefix
  (`"SEVERE — …"`) — is rejected as stringly-typed: the repository's
  established pattern for mechanical checkability is typed structure
  (`FactContribution`), not string parsing.

**Recommendation (NOT RATIFIED): C-2.** It is the minimal representation
that carries all three acceptance criteria (band, traceability, detail)
without redundancy, it reuses the FactContribution linkage precedent, and —
as amended in Rev. 2 — its criterion-typed entries are what make the WD-4
evidence ceiling computable without any semantic engine. C-1 fails AC-2;
C-3 buys illusory compatibility at the cost of a permanent dual
representation.

### WD-4 / Decision D — The evidence ceiling: the maximum defensible weight
*(the core governance decision — REWORKED IN REV. 2)*

**Governing principle (Project Owner direction, 2026-09-14):**

> **Facts establish an evidence ceiling; they do not automatically
> determine the asserted weight.**

The system **verifies**: *Can the available Facts support the claimed
rating?*
The system **never infers**: *The available Facts automatically imply this
rating.*

Consequences that every design below must respect: **more independent
sources must not automatically mean higher severity**; corroboration may
raise *confidence* in a claim without changing the *severity of the
problem* (Example 5); the engine never derives, defaults or upgrades a
rating (N-4).

**Revision note.** Rev. 1 of this section recommended a corroboration
ladder mapping independent-source count to the maximum band (2 → MINOR,
3 → MODERATE, ≥4 → SEVERE). **The Project Owner explicitly rejected that
model** — it treats corroboration as a semantic severity classifier, which
is exactly the conflation the principle above forbids. The ladder is
retained below **only as history**, as Design D-A, clearly labelled
REJECTED; it must not be read as a live option.

#### WD-4.1 Four concepts the model must keep distinct

| # | Concept | Question it answers | Where it lives | Ruled by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Evidence sufficiency** | May this Problem — and each weight claim on it — stand at all? | S-4 floor; engine refusal `INSUFFICIENT_SOURCES`; per-claim attestation (D-B) | S-4 (ratified) |
| 2 | **Evidence corroboration** | How many independent sources attest? | N-16 `independent_source_count` (distinct ACTIVE-Evidence independence keys) | N-16 (ratified) |
| 3 | **Maximum defensible weight — the evidence ceiling** | What is the highest severity/frequency band the available Facts can support? | Derived per rating from criterion declarations + citations + attestation | **this decision (WD-4)** |
| 4 | **Inferer-supplied weight rating** | What band does the inferer actually assert? | `severity`/`frequency` ratings | N-4 (ratified) |

**The load-bearing separation:** corroboration (concept 2) feeds
**confidence** — R-3/S-2 already implement this: more independent sources
raise `evidential_support` and hence confidence — and it feeds **sufficiency**
(concept 1, S-4's binary floor). It does **not** feed the band. The ceiling
(concept 3) is a function of **what the linked Facts are declared to
evidence — content — and whether that content is adequately attested**,
never of how many sources there are. Concept 4 is the inferer's assertion,
which must lie at or below the ceiling and is never raised by the system.

The mechanical measures and their roles (none is a band selector):

| Measure | Definition | Supplier | Role in the ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fact count | number of Fact objects in `supporting_facts` | inferer (hypothesis shape) | **none** — more Facts ≠ more weight |
| Evidence count | distinct Evidence objects beneath those Facts' attachments | engine-derived | P-I4's existing bound 2 only; **not** a ceiling input (pre-independence-grouping) |
| `independent_source_count` | distinct `independence_key` groups of ACTIVE Evidence beneath the supporting Facts (N-16 Tier-1) | engine-derived | **uniform per-claim sufficiency boolean only** (D-B): is this weight claim attested by ≥2 independent sources — yes/no, identical for every band. Never selects or differentiates bands |
| `evidential_support` | S-2 composite support of the contributing Facts (P6-bounded) | engine-derived | **none** — grounding quality; flows to confidence (R-3) exactly as today (Example 4) |
| rating band + criterion declarations | the asserted band; the per-Fact criterion claims | **inferer (N-4)** | the value being bounded / the content that sets the ceiling |

#### WD-4.2 The criteria vocabulary (DRAFT, from WD-1/WD-2)

The ceiling is expressed over the band criteria of WD-1/WD-2 — the
criterion ↔ band bijection:

| Severity criterion | Band | Frequency criterion | Band |
|---|---|---|---|
| `RECOVERABLE_FRICTION` | `MINOR` | `OCCURRENCE` | `EPISODIC` |
| `COMPOUNDING_COST` | `MODERATE` | `REPETITION` | `RECURRING` |
| `IRREVERSIBLE_HARM` | `SEVERE` | `CONTINUITY` | `PERSISTENT` |

A criterion is a **content claim**: "the cited Fact explicitly evidences
this". Criteria are observable (S-01 style), and — per WD-1/WD-2 — are
stated over what the evidence says, not over how much evidence there is.

#### WD-4.3 Design D-A — Corroboration ladder (REJECTED — Project Owner, 2026-09-14; retained as history only)

*The Rev. 1 recommended model. Retained so the rejected alternative is on
record (TEMPLATE field 2); it is not a live option.*

- **Rule (historical):** maximum band = f(independent_source_count):
  2 → `MINOR`, 3 → `MODERATE`, ≥4 → `SEVERE` (frequency analogous).
  Engine-derived count alone set the ceiling; no content input.
- **Why rejected:** (i) it answers "how corroborated is this problem?" and
  pretends that answers "how bad is this problem?" — a semantic
  category error the Owner has rejected; (ii) **violates Example 2**:
  four or five independent sources confirming a *minor* problem would
  make `SEVERE` assertable; (iii) **violates Example 5**: corroboration
  would move the weight axis instead of the confidence axis, collapsing
  the very distinction R-3 exists to maintain; (iv) false precision —
  three numeric rungs lending quantitative veneer to an interpretive
  judgement, against B-37's warning; (v) it *infers* weight from
  evidence ("the Facts automatically imply this much severity") rather
  than *verifying* a supplied rating against an evidence ceiling.
- **What is salvaged from it:** nothing for the ceiling. The insight that
  a rating must be *bounded* survives in every design below; the
  count→band mapping does not.

#### WD-4.4 Design D-B — Declared-criterion ceiling with uniform claim attestation (RECOMMENDED, DRAFT)

| Property | D-B |
|---|---|
| **Exact inputs** | (i) the rating's asserted `band`; (ii) the rating's `justification` entries (WD-3): each cites one supporting Fact and declares the band criterion that Fact evidences; (iii) the independence keys of ACTIVE Evidence beneath the *cited* Facts (engine-derived, N-16 Tier-1, same resolution machinery as today) |
| **Deterministic rule** | For each criterion c: **c is defensible iff the entries declaring c cite Facts spanning ≥2 distinct independence keys** (S-4's own floor value, applied to the claim). **Ceiling = the highest defensible criterion's band.** The rating is acceptable iff (a) the asserted band's own criterion is declared and defensible (equivalently: asserted band ≤ ceiling, with its own backing), (b) every cited fact_ref ∈ `supporting_facts` (WD-3 subset rule), (c) detail and justification non-empty. Severity and frequency are checked independently; one refusal per attempt, first failing axis in evaluation order (severity, then frequency), detail naming the axis |
| **Maximum defensible band** | The highest band whose criterion is declared with two-source attestation. | **Role of independent sources** | A **uniform boolean sufficiency floor per weight claim** — identical for `MINOR`, `MODERATE`, `SEVERE` and every frequency band; it never selects or differentiates bands. Justification for using it at all: S-4's own ratified reasoning — "an inference of deficiency from a single source is that source's opinion" — applied to the weight claim itself: a severe-harm *characterisation* attested by one source is that source's characterisation |

| **Role of evidential_support** | **None.** Support measures grounding quality of the claim and flows to confidence (S-2 → R-3) exactly as today (Example 4) |
| **False precision** | Low — no continuous thresholds; ordinal content criteria; the only number is S-4's already-ratified floor (2), used as a boolean |
| **N-4 compatibility** | ✓ The engine never derives, defaults or upgrades a band. It verifies: "can the cited, two-source-attested evidence support the claimed rating?" It never infers: "the Facts imply this band." Band, criteria and citations are all inferer-supplied |
| **S-2 compatibility** | ✓ Evidential support untouched; not a ceiling input; P-I4's bound 3 unchanged |
| **S-4 compatibility** | ✓ Same measure (independence keys), same floor value (2), same role (sufficiency — a floor, not a gradient). No band is treated differently |
| **N-16 compatibility** | ✓ Tier-1 key derivation reused as-is (restricted to the cited subset); non-ACTIVE Evidence contributes no key; when T02.1.3 independence grouping lands, syndicated sources collapse and attestation tightens automatically |
| **P-I4 compatibility** | ✓ This *is* P-I4's ordinal completion: "weight never asserted beyond what Facts support" = never beyond the declared, cited, adequately-attested criterion. The three numeric bounds are unchanged |
| **Implementation complexity** | Moderate — typed criterion on justification entries (WD-3); subset check (already required by WD-3); key-span check over cited Facts (engine already resolves keys); P-V4 form checks; engine ceiling gate + one N-10 stage; P-I4 detective re-derivation. No new measures, no new module |
| **Failure modes** | (i) **Declaration fidelity** — the inferer declares `IRREVERSIBLE_HARM` for a Fact that does not show it. The engine cannot read content (no NLP/LLM — M-67 open, §14 non-goal). Mitigations: the declaration is explicit, typed and per-Fact (no accidental over-claim); fully traceable for F-A1 audit sampling; visible in the explanation skeleton (N-13); re-checked by the P-I4 detective over stored objects. Residual risk accepted and recorded. (ii) A genuinely severe problem whose harm category is attested by only one source (the second source attests the problem, not the harm) cannot reach `SEVERE` — **accepted consequence**, exactly Example 1's reading: severity needs the *harm itself* evidenced by two strong independent sources. (iii) Criterion boundary disputes (B-37's accepted trade-off), mitigated by observable criteria + anchors |
| **Examples** | The five semantic tests below, all satisfied |

**The five semantic tests (mandatory test cases, §11):**

1. **Two strong independent sources CAN support a severe problem when the
   Facts explicitly evidence severe harm.** F1 (Evidence E_a, key a) and
   F2 (Evidence E_b, key b) both evidence irreversible harm. Severity
   justification: entries (F1, `IRREVERSIBLE_HARM`), (F2,
   `IRREVERSIBLE_HARM`) — the criterion is declared with citations
   spanning keys {a, b} ≥ 2 → `SEVERE` defensible; the inferer asserts
   `SEVERE` → **accepted**. Two sources were enough *because of what the
   Facts say*, not because of the count.
2. **Four or five independent sources confirming a minor problem must NOT
   automatically make it severe.** F1…F5 across five keys, all evidencing
   only recoverable friction. Only `RECOVERABLE_FRICTION` is defensible →
   ceiling `MINOR`. Asserting `SEVERE` → the `SEVERE` criterion is not
   declared+attested → **refused** (`WEIGHT_EXCEEDS_EVIDENCE`), five
   sources notwithstanding. (If the inferer *mis-declares*
   `IRREVERSIBLE_HARM` against friction-only Facts, the engine cannot see
   the mismatch — that is declaration-fidelity failure mode (i), carried
   by audit, not by counts.)
3. **Five Facts derived from only two independence relationships must not
   become five independent sources.** F1…F5 whose attachments resolve to
   Evidence sharing keys {a, b}: P-I4's existing bounds 1–2 already cap
   the declared `independent_source_count` at 2, and the ceiling is
   unaffected by there being five Facts — what matters is which criteria
   the cited Facts evidence and the key-span of those citations (at most
   {a, b}). No band gains anything from the Fact count.
4. **High evidential_support alone must not increase severity.** Support
   0.95 over friction-only content → ceiling stays `MINOR`; support is not
   a ceiling input; it flows to confidence (S-2 → R-3) as today.
5. **More corroboration may increase confidence in a claim without
   changing the underlying severity.** The same friction-evidenced problem
   corroborated by five keys instead of two: `evidential_support` (and
   hence confidence) rises with source diversity; the severity ceiling is
   **unchanged** — it was never a function of the count. Severity and
   confidence remain orthogonal axes, exactly as R-3 draws them.

### WD-5 / Decision E — Versioned weight changes
*(REWORKED IN REV. 2 for consistency with the WD-4 evidence ceiling)*

The IOM makes weight revision a versioning trigger ("Versioned as
supporting Facts accumulate or weight is revised"), and R-1 makes any
attribute change a new version. The open question is what evidence a
**weight change** requires on the versioned (reformulation) path.

**Proposed rules per case (Option E-1, RECOMMENDED — strict
additional-support for increases):**

| Successor vs predecessor | New version required? | Additional supporting Facts required? |
|---|---|---|
| Weight unchanged (both bands, details, citations) | Not by weight alone (other triggers — reformulation, added support, population change — still apply) | No |
| Weight **decreased** (either band lower) | Yes (attribute changed, R-1) | **No** — claiming less is always evidence-safe (conservative direction) |
| Weight **increased** (either band higher) | Yes | **Yes** — three conditions below |
| Severity changed only | Yes | Per-direction on that axis (increase on the severity axis → the conditions below; decrease → none) |
| Frequency changed only | Yes | Per-direction on that axis (likewise) |
| Justification/detail/citations changed, bands unchanged | Yes (attribute changed, R-1) | No — but re-cited Facts must still satisfy the WD-3 subset rule and the WD-4 ceiling |

**The central question — does increasing a band require additional
supporting Facts? — answered YES under E-1**, on three conditions:

1. **Within the evidence ceiling (WD-4):** the successor's asserted band
   satisfies the WD-4 rule on the successor's own evidence — its criterion
   is declared with citations spanning ≥2 independence keys (D-B), i.e.
   the new rating is within the maximum defensible rating.
2. **Explicit evidence-linked justification:** the entries declaring the
   higher criterion cite the specific Facts that evidence it — the
   increase itself is traceable (AC-2), not just the resulting state.
3. **Additional supporting Facts (the conservative rule):** the
   successor's supporting set contains **at least one Fact not in the
   predecessor's supporting set**, and the higher criterion's justifying
   entries cite **at least one such new Fact**.

**Why condition 3, precisely.** Under R-1, the predecessor's Facts and
their content are fixed. The WD-4 ceiling is a function of (available
Facts × inferer's criterion declarations) — and the declarations are
entirely the inferer's own. Over an **unchanged** Fact set, every
declaration available to the successor was equally available to the
predecessor: a later increase without new evidence is therefore grounded
in nothing new. It is either calibration drift or assertion inflation,
and the two are mechanically indistinguishable — so the conservative
system refuses both. Requiring the increase to cite new evidence makes
every upward weight movement traceable to something that was not in hand
before. This is the same reasoning the platform has already ratified for
population widening (P-I3, T04.1.3: a wider claim requires wider
evidence) and applies the P-I1/P-I3 confidence precedent to the weight
axis. The asymmetry — decreases need nothing — is deliberate: claiming
less can never exceed what the Facts support.

**Options compared.**

- **E-1 — strict additional-support (RECOMMENDED)**: conditions 1–3 above.
  *Accepted consequence:* in the rare case where corroboration rises
  without a new Fact (e.g. previously non-ACTIVE Evidence becoming ACTIVE
  beneath existing immutable Facts), condition 3 still refuses the
  increase; the inferer must reformulate with an added Fact.
  Ultra-conservative by design.
- **E-2 — ceiling-only.** Any version may assert any rating within its own
  WD-4 ceiling; no additional-Fact requirement. *Rejected:* a successor
  over an unchanged supporting set could declare and assert a higher band
  than its predecessor claimed from the *same* Facts — weight exceeding
  what those Facts were held to support moments earlier, with no new
  grounding; indistinguishable from assertion inflation (see the argument
  above).
- **E-3 — was-below-ceiling exception.** Allow an increase without new
  Facts when the predecessor's asserted band was below the ceiling of its
  own facts. *Rejected:* the predecessor's ceiling depends on *its own
  declarations*, which the same inferer controls — "was below ceiling" is
  self-inflicted and unfalsifiable, so the exception is a loophole, not a
  rule.

**Worked example (rewritten for the ceiling model).** Predecessor:
`severity=MINOR` over {F1, F2} (keys a, b; friction declared and
two-source attested). Successor over the **same** Facts asserting
`MODERATE` → refused: condition 3 fails (no new Fact), proposed stage
`WEIGHT_INCREASED_WITHOUT_SUPPORT`; the predecessor remains ACTIVE and
nothing is written. Successor over {F1, F2, F3} where F3 (new key c)
evidences a compounding cost: entries (F2, `COMPOUNDING_COST`), (F3,
`COMPOUNDING_COST`) span keys {b, c} ≥ 2 → `MODERATE` is within the
ceiling (condition 1), the increase cites its evidence (condition 2), and
F3 is new and cited (condition 3) → **accepted**; predecessor →
SUPERSEDED. A successor asserting `MINOR` over the same wider evidence is
also accepted — below-ceiling ratings are never upgraded.

## 8. The four concepts, normative (once ratified)

See §WD-4.1. In force after ratification: **(1) sufficiency** — S-4's
independence floor, applied to the object and (per D-B) to each weight
claim; **(2) corroboration** — the N-16 independence-key count, feeding
confidence (S-2 → R-3) and nothing else; **(3) the evidence ceiling** —
the highest band whose criterion is declared with adequately attested
citations, derived per rating, the upper bound for acceptance; **(4) the
inferer-supplied rating** — always at or below the ceiling, never derived,
never upgraded. The engine **verifies** that the available Facts can
support the claimed rating; it **never infers** a rating from the
available Facts. Fact count, Evidence count and `evidential_support` are
never ceiling inputs.

## 13. Expected risks

- **Declaration fidelity (new, the principal residual risk):** the engine
  verifies the *form* and *attestation* of criterion declarations, not
  their truth — no NLP/LLM is in scope (M-67 open; §14). A mis-declared
  criterion is structurally indistinguishable from an honest one at write
  time. Mitigations: typed, explicit, per-Fact declarations (no accidental
  over-claim); full traceability for F-A1 audit sampling; explanation
  skeleton visibility (N-13); P-I4 detective re-checks; the residual risk
  is recorded as accepted in the ratification act.
- **Fixture migration breadth** (largest mechanical risk): every free-text
  severity/frequency fixture changes type under WD-3 C-2 — mitigated by
  mechanical migration and the information-preserving `detail` field.
- **P-V4 detail text change**: breaks any test pinning the current text —
  only `test_no_scale_is_asserted`, which self-documents its supersession.
- **Boundary disputes** on criterion placement (B-37's accepted trade-off):
  mitigated by observable criteria + anchors + audit, not by more bands.
- **Downstream expectations**: P5 frequency-inflation narrative
  (`pattern.py`) and `solution.py`'s citation of M-12 openness are aware
  of but do not depend on this change; no P5 code is modified.
- **Versioned-gate interaction**: two new gates (ceiling + weight-change)
  compose with the T04.1.2/T04.1.3 gates on the same path; ordering must
  keep each refusal atomic and single-stage (N-10: one stage per attempt).
